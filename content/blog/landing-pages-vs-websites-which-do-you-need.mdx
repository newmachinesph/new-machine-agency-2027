"Do I need a website or a landing page?" is one of the most common questions we get, and the confusion is completely understandable. From the outside they look like the same thing: a page on the internet with your name on it. Under the hood, they're built for different jobs, and using one where you needed the other is a quiet, expensive mistake.

Get the choice right and every peso you spend on traffic works harder. Get it wrong and you either send paid clicks into a maze or spend months building depth you didn't need yet.

## The difference in one sentence

A **website** is your business's home. It explains everything you do, to everyone who might care, and it's built to be explored. A **landing page** is a specialist. It convinces one type of visitor to take one specific action, and it's built to be finished, not browsed.

Everything else follows from that difference. A website invites wandering; a landing page removes every reason to wander.

## When a landing page is the right call

- **You're running ads.** Sending paid traffic to your homepage is like paying for a taxi and never telling the driver where to go. Ad clicks should land on a page built around exactly what the ad promised, with the same words and the same offer, so the visitor never feels handed off.
- **You're launching something new.** A new service, a seasonal offer, a one-off event. A landing page validates demand fast, without you having to touch or restructure your main site to do it.
- **You need speed.** A good landing page ships in one to two weeks. If your campaign starts this month, that turnaround is the difference between launching on time and launching late.
- **You want clean measurement.** One page, one goal, one conversion number. No competing links, no navigation menu pulling attention away, and no noise to explain away when you read the results.

## When you need a full website

- **People are checking you out.** Referrals, investors, and corporate clients will look for an About page, your services, and some proof of a track record. A single page feels thin the moment a serious buyer starts doing their homework on you.
- **You offer several distinct services.** Each service deserves its own space, its own keywords, and its own path to inquiry, rather than being flattened into one paragraph that serves none of them well.
- **You want search traffic.** Ranking on Google requires depth: service pages, articles, and clear answers to the questions your customers actually type in. Landing pages rarely rank on their own. Websites are built to.
- **You're building a brand, not just a funnel.** A website compounds over time. Every page you add is an asset that keeps working long after you've published it, quietly pulling in visitors you never paid for.

## The answer for most growing businesses: both, staged

In practice, it's rarely one or the other. The pattern we recommend most often is to build them in sequence rather than all at once:

1. **Start with the [website](/services/website-design/).** A focused one, not a sprawling one. Five great pages beat fifteen mediocre ones, and a tight site is faster to build, cheaper to maintain, and easier for a visitor to navigate.
2. **Add [landing pages](/services/landing-pages/) per campaign.** Each ad campaign or offer gets its own dedicated page, linked to your site but stripped down and built purely to convert the traffic you're paying to send it.

The website builds trust while you sleep; the landing pages harvest the demand your ads create. They're not competitors fighting for the same budget. They're teammates, each doing the job the other one can't.
